### Question 2. How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P?

B = MAP means Behaviour = Motivation + Ability + Prompt.

BJ Fogg explains that these three things work together to make a behaviour happen.

- **M – Motivation:** How much we want to do something. For example, I may be motivated to learn Java because I want to improve my programming skills.
- **A – Ability:** How easy or difficult it is to perform the habit. If I plan to study for five hours every day, it may be difficult to maintain. Starting with just 10 minutes makes it easier.
- **P – Prompt:** A reminder or signal that tells us when to perform the habit. For example, keeping my Java notebook on my study table reminds me to practise.

I think the best way to use this formula is to make the habit simple enough that I don't have to depend on motivation every day. When the habit is easy and I have a proper reminder, I'm more likely to do it regularly.

### Question 3. Why is it important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?

Celebrating after completing a habit helps our brain connect that action with a positive feeling. It doesn't have to be a big celebration. Even saying "Good job" to myself or feeling happy after completing a small task is enough.

For example, if I complete my daily coding practice, I can take a moment to appreciate that I actually did it instead of immediately worrying about how much more I need to study.

This is important because sometimes we focus only on the final result and forget to appreciate our small efforts. Celebrating small achievements makes us feel good about the process and encourages us to repeat the behaviour. That's why BJ Fogg says that what we celebrate becomes a habit.

### Question 5. What is the book's perspective about Identity?

According to *Atomic Habits*, real behaviour change starts with changing the way we see ourselves. Instead of focusing only on what we want to achieve, we should think about the kind of person we want to become.

For example, instead of saying "I want to learn programming", I can start thinking of myself as someone who practises programming regularly.

Every time I complete a small coding task, I'm giving myself proof that I'm becoming that kind of person. Over time, these small actions help build a stronger identity. I understood that achieving a goal is good, but becoming a person who follows good habits is even more valuable.

### Question 6. Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do.

The book explains that we should reduce the effort required to perform a habit. The easier an action is, the more likely we are to do it.

One way is to prepare everything in advance. For example, if I want to practise coding every morning, I can keep my laptop ready and decide which problem I will solve the next day. This saves time and avoids unnecessary distractions.

Another idea is to start with a very small action. Instead of planning to study for two hours immediately, I can begin with just five or ten minutes. Once I get comfortable with the habit, I can slowly increase the time.

The main idea is to remove unnecessary difficulties so that doing the habit becomes easier than avoiding it.

### Question 7. Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do.

To reduce a bad habit, we should increase the effort required to perform it. If something takes more steps or becomes less convenient, we are less likely to do it.

For example, if I spend too much time scrolling through Instagram or YouTube, I can log out of those accounts, turn off notifications or keep my phone away while studying.

This creates a little difficulty before I can access those apps. That small extra effort can help me control the habit.

I think this approach is useful because instead of depending completely on self-control, we can change our surroundings to make unwanted habits less convenient.

### Question 8. Pick one habit that you would like to do more of. What steps can you take to make the cue obvious, the habit attractive or easy, and the response satisfying?

One habit I would like to improve is daily coding practice. Sometimes I plan to solve coding problems, but I end up postponing them because of distractions or a lack of motivation.

To improve this habit, I can make a few changes.

First, I will keep my laptop and coding notebook ready on my study table so that they remind me to practise. I will try to connect coding practice with an activity I already do regularly, such as starting my study session after breakfast.

Instead of setting a big target, I will start by solving at least one coding problem every day. If I feel comfortable, I can continue with more problems.

To make it attractive, I can choose problems related to topics I enjoy learning. After completing a problem, I will appreciate my effort and mark that day as completed on a habit tracker.

I believe this will help me become more consistent without feeling pressured to achieve too much every day.

### Question 9. Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of. What steps can you take to make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, or the response unsatisfying?

One habit I would like to reduce is unnecessary mobile phone usage, especially scrolling through social media and watching random YouTube videos during study time.

To control this, I can keep my phone away from my study table and turn off unnecessary notifications. I can also log out of social media accounts so that accessing them requires extra effort.

Another thing I can do is set app timers and avoid using my phone during study sessions. Whenever I feel like checking it, I can remind myself that I'm interrupting my concentration for something that can wait.

I can also track how much time I spend on these apps every day. Seeing the actual screen time may help me realise how much time I'm wasting.

I don't expect to stop this habit completely in one day, but reducing it little by little will give me more time to focus on learning and other useful activities.
