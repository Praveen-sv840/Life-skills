### Question 2. How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P?

B = MAP means Behaviour = Motivation + Ability + Prompt. These three elements help us perform a behaviour.

- **M – Motivation:** How much we want to do something, like learning Java to improve my programming skills.
- **A – Ability:** How easy the habit is to perform. Starting with 10 minutes of study is easier than planning five hours.
- **P – Prompt:** A reminder that tells us when to do something, like keeping my notebook on the study table.

By making habits simple and having proper reminders, we can follow them without depending too much on motivation.

### Question 3. Why is it important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?

Celebrating after completing a habit creates a positive feeling and encourages our brain to repeat that behaviour. Even a small appreciation like saying "Good job" to myself after completing coding practice can make a difference.

Instead of focusing only on big results, we should appreciate small achievements. This helps us enjoy the process and makes it easier to continue the habit.

### Question 5. What is the book's perspective about Identity?

According to *Atomic Habits*, changing our identity is an important part of building better habits. Instead of saying "I want to learn programming", we should start seeing ourselves as someone who practises programming regularly.

Every small action gives us proof of the person we are becoming. This shows that building a good identity is more valuable than simply achieving a goal.

### Question 6. Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do.

The book explains that we should reduce the effort needed to perform a habit. For example, keeping my laptop ready and deciding which coding problem to solve in advance makes studying easier.

We can also start with small actions instead of setting difficult targets. Once the habit becomes comfortable, we can slowly increase the effort.

### Question 7. Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

To reduce a bad habit, we should make it less convenient and increase the effort required to perform it. For example, if I spend too much time on Instagram or YouTube, I can turn off notifications and keep my phone away while studying.

These small changes make distractions harder to access and help us avoid depending completely on self-control.

### Question 8. Pick one habit that you would like to do more of. What steps can you take to make the cue obvious, the habit attractive or easy, and the response satisfying?

One habit I would like to improve is daily coding practice. Sometimes I postpone it because of distractions or a lack of motivation.

I can keep my laptop ready on the study table and start practising after breakfast. Instead of setting a big target, I'll begin by solving at least one coding problem daily. After completing it, I'll mark the day as completed on a habit tracker and appreciate my effort.

### Question 9. Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of. What steps can you take to make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, or the response unsatisfying?

One habit I want to reduce is unnecessary mobile phone usage, especially scrolling through social media and watching random YouTube videos during study time.

I can keep my phone away from my study table, turn off unnecessary notifications and set app timers. I can also track my daily screen time to understand how much time I'm spending on these apps. By reducing this habit gradually, I can spend more time focusing on learning.
