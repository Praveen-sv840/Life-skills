# Listening and Active Communication

### 1. Active Listening

**Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?**

1. I should give my full attention to the person speaking instead of thinking about something else.
2. I should avoid distractions and focus on the speaker and the topic.
3. I should not interrupt the speaker. I should wait until they finish before responding.
4. I should use phrases like "Tell me more" or "What happened next?" to encourage the person to continue speaking.
5. I should show that I am listening through my body language, such as maintaining eye contact and nodding.
6. I should take notes during important conversations so that I don't miss important information.
7. I should repeat or explain what I understood in my own words to make sure I understood the message correctly.

### 2. Reflective Listening

**Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?**

According to Fisher's model, reflective listening means understanding what the other person is saying instead of just hearing their words. I should try to understand their feelings and point of view without judging them.

The main things I should follow are:

1. I should listen more and talk less. I should give the other person enough time to explain their thoughts.
2. I should understand the person's feelings along with the information they are sharing.
3. I should repeat or explain their message in my own words to check whether I understood it correctly.
4. I should try to understand the situation from the speaker's point of view instead of thinking only about my own opinion.
5. I should accept different opinions and avoid becoming defensive when someone disagrees with me.
6. I should show genuine interest and respond with understanding rather than ignoring their feelings.

For example, during a technical discussion in a team, I should take notes about what everyone says. After the discussion, I can share those notes with my teammates and ask them to correct anything I misunderstood. This helps everyone work with the same understanding.

### 3. Reflection

**Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?**

One of my main problems is that I sometimes think about my reply while the other person is still speaking. Because of this, I miss some important points. I also get distracted by my phone or other things happening around me. Sometimes, when someone takes a long time to explain something, I lose interest. Another problem is that I make assumptions before the person finishes explaining. If someone disagrees with my opinion, I sometimes focus more on defending myself than understanding what they are saying.

**Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?**

I can improve my listening by keeping my phone aside when someone is talking to me. I should allow others to finish speaking instead of interrupting them. I can also ask questions whenever I don't understand something rather than making my own assumptions. During meetings and technical discussions, I should take notes so I don't forget important details. I should also try to understand other people's opinions even when they are different from mine.

### 4. Types of Communication

**Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

I usually become passive when I don't want to hurt someone or get into an argument. For example, when my friends make a plan that I don't really like, I sometimes agree with them instead of telling them what I actually want. Similarly, during group projects, if I disagree with someone's idea, I sometimes stay quiet because I don't want to create problems. I feel that keeping quiet is easier at that moment, but sometimes I regret not expressing my opinion.

**Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?**

I sometimes become aggressive when I am frustrated or when things don't happen as expected. For example, if I explain something several times and the other person still doesn't understand, I may lose my patience and raise my voice. I also react aggressively when someone repeatedly interrupts me or doesn't take my opinion seriously. Later, I realise that I could have explained the same thing in a calmer way.

**Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?**

Sometimes, when I am upset with someone, I don't tell them directly. Instead, I use sarcasm or stop talking to them. For example, if a friend forgets something important, I might say, "You never remember anything," instead of explaining why I am disappointed. Sometimes I also avoid replying properly when I am angry. I realise that this creates misunderstandings because the other person may not know what is actually bothering me.

**Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?**

After going through the videos, I understood that assertive communication means expressing what I feel and need clearly while also respecting other people's feelings. I can improve my communication in a few ways.

1. First, I should understand my own feelings before speaking. Instead of blaming someone, I should explain how their actions make me feel.
2. I should understand what I actually need instead of expecting others to guess it.
3. I can start practising with simple situations, like asking my friend to lower the music when I am studying or telling someone politely when I disagree.
4. I should pay attention to my body language and tone. I should speak confidently without shouting or sounding rude.
5. If something bothers me, I should speak about it early instead of keeping it inside until I become frustrated.
6. I should also respect the other person's opinion. Being assertive doesn't mean that I am always right or that others must agree with me.

For example, if a teammate is not completing their assigned work, instead of getting angry or staying silent, I can tell them that the delay is affecting the project and ask them to complete their part on time.

I understood that I don't have to agree with everyone just to avoid arguments, and I don't have to be rude to make my point. I should be able to express what I think clearly and listen to what others have to say. I want to practise this more in my daily conversations, college projects and future workplace.
