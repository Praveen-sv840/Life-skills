### Question 1. What kind of behaviour can cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment can happen in different ways at the workplace. It is not only about physical contact. Making unwanted comments about someone's appearance, telling sexual jokes, staring at someone in an uncomfortable way, or sending inappropriate messages and pictures can also be considered harassment. Asking someone repeatedly for a date or sexual favours even after they have said no is also unacceptable.

Sometimes, a person may use their position or authority to threaten someone or promise job benefits in return for sexual favours. Sharing someone's private pictures or making unwanted sexual remarks can also make them feel unsafe. I understood that even one incident should be taken seriously. It doesn't matter whether the person is a colleague, manager, or senior employee. Everyone should respect other people's boundaries.

### Question 2. What would you do if you faced or witnessed one or repeated incidents?

If I faced this kind of situation, I would first try to stay calm and make it clear that the behaviour is not acceptable, if I felt safe doing so. I would keep any messages, emails, or other evidence related to the incident and note down what happened, including the date and place.

I would report the matter to HR or the Internal Committee instead of keeping it to myself. If I witnessed something happening to another person, I would try to support them and encourage them to report it. I would also avoid spreading the matter among other colleagues because it is a personal issue and should be handled confidentially.

I believe everyone has the right to work in a safe and respectful environment. We should treat our colleagues properly, respect their personal boundaries, and never stay silent when we see someone being harassed.
